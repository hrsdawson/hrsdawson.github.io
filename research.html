Research

Interests

I have varied research interests but the Southern Ocean and Antarctic margins have my heart. Within these regions, I'm interested in large-scale circulation and dynamics, physical-biogeochemical interactions, the response to and impacts of climate change, large scale cryosphere-ocean interactions, and more recently - drivers of sea ice variability. Above all though, I love collaborative science. If you'd like to work on something together, please reach out.

Current research

I currently work as a postdoctoral research associate at the Institute for Marine and Antarctic Studies in Nipaluna/Hobart. My postdoc research focuses on understanding the drivers of Antarctic sea ice, which has shown unprecedented variablity in recent years. To do this, I run coupled climate models and investigate the response of the sea ice system to perturbed forcings. By doing so we hope to improve our understanding of how sea ice might evolve over the coming decades.

PhD thesis

I completed my PhD at the Climate Change Research Centre at UNSW in 2024. In my PhD I used particle tracking experiments and ocean-sea ice models to investigate ocean connectivity and future change around the Antarctic margins. During this time I gained experience running global ocean-sea ice models, conducting Lagrangian particle tracking experiments, using high performance computing facilities, and an array of data analysis techniques.
